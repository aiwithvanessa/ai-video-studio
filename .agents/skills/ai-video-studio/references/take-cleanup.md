# Clean up an uncut recording

Use when the user requests bad-take/false-start cleanup. An accepted cleanup brief authorizes ordinary local analysis, selection, preparation and draft rendering; do not ask permission for each cut. Preserve originals. Do not silently apply cleanup to style references or normal footage without that intent.

Offer an optional script with the editing instructions: attach or paste it; rough wording is fine. Never require a script for unscripted speech. Follow [script-assisted review](script-assisted-review.md). A script describes intended points, not what was actually said or when it was said. Assess paraphrases and complete repeated takes across the whole recording, not only adjacent unfinished prefixes.

Import footage and inspect the full intended section with studio intake <id> --source <asset> --duration full. Do not truncate analysis to the default first15seconds. Run prepare-speech on that full section before take selection. It executes recognition and acoustic alignment; source timing estimates alone cannot pass cleanup.

studio cleanup-analyze <id> writes an immutable proposal and readable text under the video's generated/cleanup folder. Its deterministic detector handles only a repeated unfinished prefix followed by a longer take after a pause. Codex must also read the full transcript, inspect candidates/joins and select coherent takes semantically. Later complete takes are a useful default, not proof of better delivery. Complete repeated lines, similar wording, emphasis and performance quality need assessment. Do not remove every pause, um, repeated word or profanity. Retain genuinely ambiguous content and flag it. Never claim listening from text or frames alone.

The agent saves a new plan: schemaVersion1, sourceSha256, transcriptSha256, sectionStart, sourceDurationFrames, fps30, cuts [{startFrame,endFrame,reason,evidenceWordIndices?}], warnings. Use half-open source-section frames, exact source/transcript hashes and a reason for every omission. The user does not need frames or JSON. Keep unique points; explain semantic choices in a plain-language report. No global timestamp shifts, volume snapping, invented word boundaries or hidden repairs.

studio cleanup-apply <id> --file <public-relative plan> uses local FFmpeg trim/atrim/concat with one thread to create a separate H.264/yuv420p edit. Audio/video use the same frame-derived ranges, with explicit audio-stream offset handling. A video/media-origin mismatch beyond one frame fails clearly. Frame count, duration, dimensions and audio presence are checked before changing state. At most40 kept ranges per pass are supported. This is a single-recording preparation workflow, not another nonlinear editor.

Caption intervals are mapped mathematically into the kept timeline, with original text/order and source indices retained. Cuts crossing an interval fail; no clamping. Reports list removed intervals and reasons. The source transcript, raw output, previous plans/media remain. Cleanup clears export approval. Apply cleanup before timed graphics/effects/sounds; applying/restoring with such cues fails instead of silently misaligning them.

If word estimates are invalid, preserve them. Prepare-speech may use their text/search windows for acoustic alignment, never as final intervals. Phrase-mode raw outputs remain diagnostic only, not a caption/cleanup fallback. The aligner splits text into words and computes intervals from audio, not by dividing segment durations. Unsupported windows or invalid alignment block processing. Never remove actual speech to satisfy a validator. Correction proposals remain separate and honestly labelled.

Render studio draft <id>, show the playable result and removal summary, and identify uncertain wording/joins. The final export still requires approval of the current draft. Accept “keep the first take instead”, “put that line back” or “the cut is too tight”. studio cleanup-restore <id> restores the original section and transcript while preserving generated comparisons; create a new plan and reapply without downloading/transcribing again. Do not reset unrelated videos/brand choices.

Before another render, read back the complete retained transcript in edit order. Check for duplicated instructions, lost unique points, sentences beginning/ending mid-thought and captions inherited across a join. Inspect/listen to cut boundaries using available tools; ASR phrase bounds and structural checks alone cannot certify a cut. If listening is unavailable, record that limitation explicitly and do not call boundaries safe or delivery validated. A phrase-timed fallback may be structurally valid yet audibly wrong; do not present it as a timing fix. Do not make a rejected draft pass by substituting script wording or moving timestamps without evidence.

When recognition/timing fails, use the optional [local acoustic alignment](alignment.md) path. Preserve raw and aligned versions; select complete takes from improved recognition before remapping. An aligner cannot discover speech omitted by the recognizer or certify an invented correction.
Cleanup also checks acoustic word overlap, at least 120 ms of context around internal cuts, sentence-ending punctuation and cut-adjacent confidence. These are conservative rejection rules, not proof of semantics/listening. Retain an uncertain take rather than making a tight cut. Do not replace a unique spoken instruction with an on-screen card merely because recognition missed it. Read/listen where possible, compare the whole retained transcript and flag missing script points before rendering.

Implementation: scripts/cleanup.mjs; src/lib/cleanup.mjs; cleanup-analyze/cleanup-apply/cleanup-restore; shared EditedVideo/Captions. The automatic detector alone is not a complete bad-take selector: Codex supplies semantic choices. Synthetic pulse/timestamp tests are separate from real recognition/listening accuracy. Official filter semantics: [FFmpeg filters](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html).
